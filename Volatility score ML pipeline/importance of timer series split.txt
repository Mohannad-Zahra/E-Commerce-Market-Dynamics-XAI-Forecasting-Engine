The Time-Series Split (Forward Chaining) is the most critical part of your architecture. Without it, your model would be fundamentally broken. Here is why:

1. Eliminating "Crystal Ball" Bias (Look-ahead)
In a standard K-Fold split, the data is shuffled randomly. This means the model might train on price data from December to predict what happened in January.

The Problem: In the real world, you don't know December's prices when you are in January.
The Result: If you don't use Time-Series Split, the model will appear to have 99% accuracy because it is "cheating" by seeing the future, but it will fail completely the moment you deploy it.
2. Protecting Temporal Lags
You are using features like price_lag_7d and price_lag_14d.

These features are mathematically derived from previous days.
If you shuffle the data (K-Fold), the "future" prices of a product might leak into the "past" lags of the same product. This is called Data Leakage, and it makes the training results meaningless.
3. Handling Macro-Economic Drift (Inflation)
In the Egyptian market, the relationship between the USD rate and electronics prices changes over time (e.g., prices might react faster to currency spikes in 2026 than they did in 2025).

Time-Series Split allows you to see if the model is still accurate as you move forward in time.
It proves that your model is robust enough to handle new shocks it has never seen before.
4. Real-World Validation
When your FastAPI backend goes live, it will use today's data to predict tomorrow's volatility.

Forward Chaining is the only validation method that actually mimics how your model will be used in production. It treats the "Validation Month" as if it were the "Upcoming Month."
